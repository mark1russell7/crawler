## What PoliteCrawler is

PoliteCrawler is the user agent of [polite-crawler](../), open-source software for research and for AI assistants. A person starts it on their own computer to read pages that they or their assistant asked for. It is not a search engine, and it does not collect training data.

Many people can use the software. When the operator gave a contact, the `User-Agent` includes it. An email address of the operator also goes into the `From` header.

## What it does on your site

- **It reads robots.txt first** and obeys it, as RFC 9309 specifies.
- **It sends one request at a time** to your site, with a rest of 3 seconds or more between two requests.
- **It fetches the pages that it was asked for.** It does not follow every link of your site.
- **It obeys `Retry-After`** on a 429 or a 503.
- **It stops at a refusal.** After a challenge, a 401, a 402 or a 403, it sends no more requests to your site in that run.
- **It sends conditional requests.** With an `ETag` or a `Last-Modified` date, your server can answer `304 Not Modified` and send no body.

## What it keeps

PoliteCrawler keeps each response that it fetched in a private store on the computer of its operator: a log of each request, and the bodies. The store stays until the operator deletes it. A stored copy is the record of what a page said, so a citation can name it. The crawler does not publish the stored copies.

- **By default, it uses a stored copy for 24 hours.** In that time, a question about the page sends no request to your site. The operator can set a shorter or a longer age.
- **It reads `Cache-Control` and `Expires`.** With the age `auto`, it uses a stored copy only while your server says that the copy is fresh. `no-store` and `no-cache` make a copy stale at once.
- **It keeps a response with `no-store` too.** `no-store` changes when the crawler asks again, not what it keeps.

## How to control it

To block PoliteCrawler, add this group to your robots.txt:

```text
User-agent: PoliteCrawler
Disallow: /
```

To slow it down, add a crawl delay in seconds:

```text
User-agent: PoliteCrawler
Crawl-delay: 10
```

When the crawler fetches a page for an AI assistant, it also obeys your rules for `Claude-User`.

An application can use the software with a product token of its own. When its `User-Agent` links this page, it also obeys your rules for `PoliteCrawler`, and the stricter rules apply. An application with a bot page of its own can turn this off.

To refuse the use of your text as AI input, add a content signal. The crawler keeps such a page but holds back its text from an AI reader:

```text
User-agent: *
Content-Signal: ai-input=no
```

[Test your robots.txt](../tools/robots-txt-tester/) with the same code that the crawler uses.

To refuse the crawlers that collect training data and let the AI assistants in, [use this robots.txt](../block-ai-training-crawlers/).
