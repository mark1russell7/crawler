## What PoliteCrawler is

PoliteCrawler is the user agent of [polite-crawler](../), open-source software for research and for AI assistants. A person starts it on their own computer to read pages that they or their assistant asked for. It is not a search engine, and it does not collect training data.

Many people can use the software. When the operator gave a contact, the `User-Agent` includes it. An email address of the operator also goes into the `From` header.

## What it does on your site

- **It reads robots.txt first** and obeys it, as RFC 9309 specifies.
- **It sends one request at a time** to your site, with a rest of 3 seconds or more between two requests.
- **It fetches the pages that a person or an assistant asks for.** A small crawl or a recipe can follow a few links of your site, within a limit of pages and time. It does not follow every link of your site.
- **It checks a watched page on a schedule.** An operator can add a page to a watch list. The crawler then checks it again, at most one time in each hour by default.
- **It obeys `Retry-After`** on a 429 or a 503.
- **It stops at a refusal.** After a 401 or a 403, it sends no more requests to your site for 1 hour. After a challenge or a 402, it stops for 24 hours. The stop stays when the crawler starts again.
- **It sends conditional requests.** With an `ETag` or a `Last-Modified` date, your server can answer `304 Not Modified` and send no body. When your `ETag` changes at each response and your `Last-Modified` date does not, the crawler learns it. It then sends only the date, so a `304` comes back.

## What it keeps

PoliteCrawler keeps each response that it fetched in a private store on the computer of its operator: a log of each request, and the bodies. The store stays until the operator deletes it. A stored copy is the record of what a page said, so a citation can name it. The crawler does not publish the stored copies.

- **By default, it uses a stored copy for 24 hours.** In that time, a question about the page sends no request to your site. The operator can set a shorter or a longer age.
- **It reads `Cache-Control` and `Expires`.** With the age `auto`, it uses a stored copy only while your server says that the copy is fresh. `no-store` and `no-cache` make a copy stale at once.
- **It keeps a response with `no-store` too.** `no-store` changes when the crawler asks again, not what it keeps.

## How to control it

To block PoliteCrawler, add this group to your robots.txt:

```text
User-agent: PoliteCrawler
Disallow: /
```

To slow it down, add a crawl delay in seconds:

```text
User-agent: PoliteCrawler
Crawl-delay: 10
```

When the crawler fetches a page for an AI assistant, it also obeys your rules for `Claude-User`.

An application can use the software with a product token of its own. When its `User-Agent` links this page, it also obeys your rules for `PoliteCrawler`, and the stricter rules apply. An application with a bot page of its own can turn this off.

To refuse the use of your text as AI input, add a content signal. The crawler keeps such a page but holds back its text from an AI reader:

```text
User-agent: *
Content-Signal: ai-input=no
```

[Test your robots.txt](../tools/robots-txt-tester/) with the same code that the crawler uses.

To refuse the crawlers that collect training data and let the AI assistants in, [use this robots.txt](../block-ai-training-crawlers/).

## Archived copies

An operator can ask the crawler for an archived copy of a page that your site refused. A page of a site that is down can get a copy too. The crawler then reads a copy from the Wayback Machine or Common Crawl, not from your site.

- **It is off by default.** The operator turns it on for each fetch.
- **It respects your current robots.txt.** By default, the crawler gives no archived copy of a page that your current robots.txt disallows for it. Only a stop, an error or a dead host gets a copy. An operator can change this on their own computer. An AI assistant cannot.
- **It respects your usage signals.** When your robots.txt or the archived response says `ai-input=no`, the crawler holds back the text of the copy from an AI reader.
- **It is marked.** A copy names its archive and its date. The crawler does not show it as a fresh fetch of your site.
- **It sends no request to your site.** A stop for your site stays a stop.

## Signed requests

An operator can give the crawler an Ed25519 key. The crawler then signs each request with HTTP Message Signatures, as the IETF Web Bot Auth draft tells. Each request then has the headers `Signature-Agent`, `Signature-Input` and `Signature`.

- `Signature-Agent` names the key directory of the operator. Your server or your CDN can get the public key there and verify the request.
- A signature identifies one operator, not each copy of polite-crawler. Each operator has their own key.
- The `User-Agent` header stays the same, with a signature or without one.
