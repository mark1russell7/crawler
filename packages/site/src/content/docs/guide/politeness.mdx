A polite crawler costs a site almost nothing, and it stops when the site says no. The package keeps these rules in its code. You cannot turn them off with an option.

## One request at a time for each host

The speed of a polite crawler is the number of hosts in progress, divided by the rest for each host. Thus the crawler keeps one request in progress for each host, and it fetches from many hosts in parallel. The [live crawl on the home page](../../) uses this scheduler.

The rest between two requests to one host is the longest of these times:

- **3 seconds** for a web page, and 1 second for a documented API.
- **5 times the duration of the last request**, to a maximum of 30 seconds. A slow server gets more rest.
- **The `Crawl-delay`** of robots.txt, to a maximum of 60 seconds.
- **The `Retry-After`** of a 429 or a 503. Without it, a random backoff that grows with each failure, to a maximum of 5 minutes.

## A refusal stops the host

A 401 or a 403 stops the host for 1 hour. A challenge page or a 402 stops it for 24 hours. The crawler does not try again with a different identity, a proxy or a browser in disguise. `Crawler.resetHosts()` ends the stops before their time.

Five errors in sequence stop a host for 1 hour. An error is no response, or a status, for example 500. Another 4xx, for example 400 or 422, is an error of the request and not of the host, so it does not count. A 429, 502, 503 or 504 does not stop the host. The host waits, for the time of `Retry-After` or for a backoff of up to 5 minutes. A `Retry-After` of more than 1 hour stops the host for that time, to a maximum of 30 days.

## An honest User-Agent

The default header is:

```text
PoliteCrawler/0.1.0 (+https://mark1russell7.github.io/crawler/bot/)
```

The link goes to [a page for site owners](../../bot/). The `contact` option adds an address to the header. An email address also goes into the `From` header, and a URL does not, because `From` holds only a mailbox. The crawler sends no contact without it.

## robots.txt first

For each web page, the crawler reads the robots.txt of the site first and obeys it, as RFC 9309 specifies. When the crawler cannot read robots.txt, because of a dead domain, a broken certificate or a server error, it fetches nothing from the site. A documented public API follows its own terms. [Test a robots.txt](../../tools/robots-txt-tester/).

## Conditional requests

When the store has a copy of a page, the request includes its `ETag` and its `Last-Modified` date. A site that answers `304 Not Modified` sends no body, and the crawler reads the stored copy again.

## Content signals

A site can state the uses of its content in robots.txt or in a header. Two formats exist: the `Content-Signal` lines of Cloudflare, and the `ai-use` of the IETF AIPREF draft. When a page refuses AI input, the crawler keeps the page but holds back its text from an AI reader. A training opt-out (`ai-train=no`) does not apply, because the crawler trains no model. The crawler also records the RSL `License` lines of robots.txt and the TDMRep reservation (`tdm-reservation` and `tdm-policy`, in a header or a `<meta>` tag) with each capture. These records do not change what it does.
